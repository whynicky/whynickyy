# whynicky® — Impression

A motion-first, 12-viewport print experiment. No project presentations, informational sections, stock imagery, or generated sculpture. The source of every major shape is the whynicky wordmark or the sheet carrying it.

1. Arrival: an oversized ink-black identity, sharply cropped on warm paper; the printed surface settles into place.
2. Peel: a diagonal curl lifts the inked sheet, revealing raised type beneath; camera swings to its edge.
3. Relief: actual extruded Inter letterforms emerge from the printing bed. The camera skims the letter edges and their long shadows, then returns to a graphic frontal composition.
4. Raster: a close-up of the print becomes a field of deliberate halftone marks; a dolly reveals the identity built from those marks. A graphic inversion changes the atmosphere.
5. Exposure: a moving light and pressure response reveal registration layers under cursor/touch. A keyboard control changes the impression. No task must be completed to continue.
6. Fold: the impression becomes one continuous accordion-folded sheet with physical creases. It opens, turns and closes with a side-lit camera pass.
7. Registration: translucent graphic films arrive from distinct depths. Offset ink copies merge into one aligned impression; camera roll resolves to level.
8. Exit: the resolved print fills a new, dark composition. One link exits to the original informational portfolio.

Pacing: frontal holds alternate with oblique movement and close-up transitions. Transformations are reversible. UI remains limited to identity, motion preference, scene navigation and the exit. Portrait cameras and typography are separately composed. Static edition retains the print language without motion.

Reference observed again in browser: lateral spatial motion, dark-to-light transition, close-up distorted typography. No reference assets, grid environment or triangular identity are reused.
